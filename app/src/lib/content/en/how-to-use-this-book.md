Welcome to this book. Before we start, please take three minutes to read this page. It tells you **where this book wants to take you, how to read it, and one mindset that will save you a lot of detours**.

This book is for **ordinary people who know their way around a computer but have no background in AI or programming**. If you can send and receive email and use a browser and office software, you can read it just fine. **You don't need to know how to code.** When we reach the chapters where you "build a project hands-on," we'll guide you to direct the AI in plain language, not make you memorize commands.


## This revision: 2026-10-22

AI products change quickly. This edition separates **changing capabilities and operating steps** from **lasting foundational concepts**. Start with tool selection in Chapter 18, Skills/MCP in Chapter 22, the practical guides in Chapters 28–29, and billing in Chapter 38. Updated chapters include primary sources and verification dates. A verification date is not a feature's release date, and availability can vary by account, region, and platform.

We also correct several oversimplifications: models can memorize some training text verbatim; embeddings are not encryption; context can be selected, compressed, or truncated; and subscription allowances differ from API bills. Follow the linked sources and evaluate tools on your own tasks instead of chasing every new model name.

## 2. Two ways to read, take what you need

You don't have to read it cover to cover. Pick the path that fits you:

- **You just want to use it:** Read **Parts One and Two** (basics + everyday use), then skip to **Part Six** (risk). That's enough to use AI smoothly and steadily.
- **You want to build things:** On top of the above, keep going through **Parts Three, Four, and Five** (tool ecosystem, then coding in practice, then six real projects). Step by step you'll build a personal website, a document tool, a meeting-notes assistant, and even connect AI to your own program.

Whichever path you take, **don't skip Part One**. It's the foundation of the whole book, and every "why use it this way" later is built on it.

## 3. How to use the extras

- **The "Quiz" at the end of each chapter:** Usually six questions, mostly concept, misconception, and scenario questions, often ending with a hands-on or observation exercise (a few chapters add other types, such as a calculation). For multiple-choice questions, the reader shows the answer and explanation after you pick an option; hands-on and observation exercises have no single right answer, so you can expand a list of points to look for. Don't just check right or wrong. The useful part is the explanation: why an answer is wrong, and when people tend to fall for it.
- **Glossary (Appendix A):** A three-language reference (Chinese, Japanese, English). Look up any word you don't know.
- **Prompt templates (Appendix B):** Fill-in-the-blank templates for everyday, work, study, coding, and other situations.
- **Trilingual (Chinese / Japanese / English):** This book comes in Chinese, Japanese, and English editions. Click "**中文 / 日本語 / English**" at the top of the reader to switch with one tap; the content matches one to one.
- The reader also supports **jumping through the table of contents on the left, full-text search, and highlighting text and writing notes**. Where you've read and what you've marked are saved automatically.

## 4. One mindset that runs through the whole book

If this book can get you to remember only two sentences, let them be these:

1. **AI is very capable, but it will "be confidently wrong."** Fluent, confident wording is not proof of correctness. Alignment training, retrieval, and verification tools can improve reliability without eliminating errors. So **always check important facts, numbers, and quotes yourself**. Treat it as a smart, handy assistant that occasionally talks nonsense, not an encyclopedia that's always right.
2. **Current context, stored history, and product memory are different things.** When you see it "remembering you," in almost every case that's **the product layer feeding your information back in each time**, not the model itself actually remembering (chapters 3 and 9 explain this fully). Once you understand this, a lot of confusion about "how did it forget?" and "how does it suddenly remember?" clears up.

> **Key point:** Read every later chapter with these two points in mind ("verify results, understand memory limits"), and you'll find that nearly all the book's methods and reminders are teaching you how to live with these two traits and use AI well and steadily.
